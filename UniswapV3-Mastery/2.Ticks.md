# Uniswap V3 — Ticks  ⭐️

## 1. What Are Ticks?

**Ticks are predefined price boundaries where liquidity can begin or end.**

They let Uniswap V3 track where active liquidity changes during a swap.

- **Tick spacing** depends on the pool's fee tier.
- Smaller spacing → more precise price boundaries.
- Larger spacing → fewer available boundaries.

## 2. Price vs. Ticks

Price moves continuously between ticks; it does not need to stop at each tick.

## 3. What Happens When a Tick Is Crossed?

Suppose three LPs have these ranges:

| LP | Price Range |
|---|---|
| Alice | $1,800–$2,200 |
| Bob | $1,900–$2,100 |
| Charlie | $2,000–$2,400 |

At $2,050, all three positions are active.

When the price crosses above $2,100, Bob's position becomes inactive, decreasing active liquidity.

**During a tick crossing, the pool updates active liquidity and continues the swap using the updated amount.** A tick crossing itself does not deposit or withdraw tokens.

### Key Takeaway

**Ticks define price boundaries. When a swap crosses an initialized tick, Uniswap V3 updates active liquidity to reflect the positions that are active at the new price.**

# Uniswap V3 — Tick-to-Price Formula ⭐️

### 1. Tick-to-Price Formula

**Formula:**

**p(i) = 1.0001ⁱ**

- **i** = tick index (integer).
- **p(i)** = price represented by that tick.
- Each increase of one tick multiplies the price by **1.0001**, approximately a **0.01% increase**.

Each tick index corresponds to a predefined price.

### 2. Tick Price Examples

| Tick Index (i) | Formula | Tick Price p(i) |
|---:|---|---:|
| -1 | 1.0001⁻¹ = 1 / 1.0001 | ≈ 0.99990001 |
| 0 | 1.0001⁰ | 1 |
| 1 | 1.0001¹ | 1.0001 |
| 2 | 1.0001² | 1.00020001 |

### 3. Positive vs. Negative Ticks

- **Negative tick (i < 0):** Price is below 1.
- **Zero tick (i = 0):** Price equals 1.
- **Positive tick (i > 0):** Price is above 1.

### 4. Current Price vs. Tick Price

The current price can move continuously between tick prices. It does not have to match a tick price exactly.

**Example:**

**1 < 1.00005 < 1.0001**

This means the current price of **1.00005** lies between tick 0 and tick 1.

- Price = **1.0000** → exactly tick 0.
- Price = **1.00005** → between ticks 0 and 1.
- Price = **1.0001** → exactly tick 1.

### 5. Tick Range

The permitted tick index range is:

**−887272 ≤ i ≤ 887272**

### Key Takeaway

Ticks are integer indices that represent predefined prices using the formula **p(i) = 1.0001ⁱ**. The current price can lie between tick prices. When the price crosses an initialized tick with a recorded liquidity change, the pool updates its active liquidity.


# Uniswap V3 — Token Price vs. Ticks ⭐️

### 1. Tick Price vs. Current Price

**Tick-to-price formula:**

`p(i) = 1.0001^i`

- **Tick:** An integer index representing a predefined price.
- **Tick price:** The price associated with a specific tick.
- **Current price:** The pool's actual price, which changes during swaps.

The current price can lie **between two tick prices**; it does not have to match a tick exactly.

**Analogy:** Ticks are like mileage markers on a road. A car moves continuously between them.

### 2. Why Is the Multiplier 1.0001?

`1.0001 = 1 + 0.0001`

`0.0001 = 0.01% = 1 basis point (bps)`

Each increase of one tick multiplies the previous tick price by **1.0001**, producing a **0.01% relative price increase**.

### 3. Relative vs. Absolute Price Change

- **Absolute change:** The numerical difference between two prices.
- **Relative change:** The difference compared with the starting price.

**Formula for neighboring ticks:**

`p(i + 1) = p(i) * 1.0001`

`Relative change = 0.01%`

The absolute difference varies with the starting price, but the relative difference remains approximately 0.01%.

### 4. Examples

| Tick transition | Starting price (approx.) | Absolute price change (approx.) | Relative change |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 10 → 11 | 1.00100045 | 0.00010010 | 0.01% |
| 100000 → 100001 | 22015.45605 | 2.20154561 | 0.01% |

**Key observation:** Higher prices have larger absolute differences between neighboring ticks, even though the percentage change remains the same.

### 5. Why Does This Matter?

Uniswap uses a proportional price step rather than a fixed absolute price difference. This allows neighboring ticks to represent consistent relative price changes across different price levels.

Ticks also mark where liquidity can be adjusted. **Crossing a tick changes active liquidity only when that initialized tick has a recorded liquidity change.**

### Quick Revision

- **Tick price:** `p(i) = 1.0001^i`
- **One tick upward:** Multiply price by `1.0001`.
- **Relative increase:** Approximately `0.01% = 1 bps`.
- **Absolute increase:** Depends on the starting price.
- **Current price:** Can lie between tick prices.
- **Liquidity adjustment:** Occurs at relevant initialized tick boundaries.


# Uniswap V3 — Tick Range, Price Curve & Current Tick ⭐️

## 1. Tick Range

A tick range defines the **lower and upper price boundaries** of a liquidity position.

- `pa` = lower tick price.
- `pb` = upper tick price.
- `p(i) = 1.0001^i` = formula for calculating a tick price.

Example:

`Tick range: (-10, 10)`

This represents the price interval between the prices of tick `-10` and tick `10`.

- Price inside the range → position's liquidity is active.
- Price outside the range → position's liquidity is inactive.

## 2. Price Curve vs. Price Number Line

Uniswap V3 price can be represented in two ways:

**X-Y curve**
- X-axis and Y-axis represent token amounts.
- Each point represents a combination of token amounts and a corresponding price.
- As the price of token X increases, the point moves up and left along the curve.

**Price number line**
- Moving right → price increases.
- Moving left → price decreases.
- Tick marks represent predefined prices.
- Current price can lie between tick marks.

**Key point:** Both diagrams represent the same price movement using different coordinate systems.

## 3. Liquidity Across a Tick Range

Liquidity positions can cover different price ranges.

Example:
- Alice: ticks `-10` to `10`.
- Bob: ticks `0` to `20`.

| Current price location | Active positions |
|---|---|
| Between ticks `-10` and `0` | Alice |
| Between ticks `0` and `10` | Alice + Bob |
| Between ticks `10` and `20` | Bob |

When the price leaves a position's range, its liquidity stops contributing to active liquidity. The position's funds are not automatically withdrawn.

## 4. Meaning of the X-Y Axes

**Uniswap V2**
- `x` = amount of token X in the pool.
- `y` = amount of token Y in the pool.
- Constant-product relationship: `x * y = k`.

**Uniswap V3**
- Liquidity positions can cover different price ranges.
- Token amounts and active liquidity vary with the current price and position ranges.
- The X-Y axes represent token amounts, not the token price directly.

## 5. Current Tick

The current tick is the tick index associated with the current price.

Conceptually, it identifies the lower tick boundary when the price lies between two consecutive tick prices.

Example:

- Tick `10` price ≈ `1.00100045`
- Tick `11` price ≈ `1.00110055`
- Current price = `1.00105`

**Current tick = 10**, because the current price lies between tick `10` and tick `11`.

- Price increases → current tick generally increases.
- Price decreases → current tick generally decreases.

## 6. Initialized Tick vs. Current Tick

**Current tick**
- Tracks the tick index associated with the current price.

**Initialized tick**
- Stores liquidity-related information for a tick boundary.
- Crossing it can change active liquidity if it has a nonzero recorded liquidity change.

Not every tick is initialized, and not every tick crossing changes active liquidity.

## 7. Smart Contract Connection

Uniswap V3 tracks the current square-root price and tick.

```solidity
uint160 sqrtPriceX96;
int24 tick;
```

- `sqrtPriceX96` = square root of the raw price ratio, scaled by `2^96`.
- `tick` = current tick index.

Raw price relationship:

`Price = (sqrtPriceX96 / 2^96)^2`

The raw ratio is `token1 / token0`. Token decimals must be considered to obtain the human-readable price.

## Quick Revision

- **Tick range:** Defines a position's lower and upper price boundaries.
- **Price curve:** Shows the relationship between token amounts.
- **Price number line:** Shows price moving between tick boundaries.
- **Active liquidity:** Liquidity available at the current price.
- **Current tick:** Tracks the tick index associated with the current price.
- **Initialized tick:** Stores liquidity-related data used during tick crossing.
- **`sqrtPriceX96`:** Represents the current square-root price in fixed-point format.

# Uniswap V3 — Current Tick and Token Decimals ⭐️

## 1. Where Is the Current Tick Stored?

## What is slot0()?


Uniswap V3 stores important information about its current trading state in a data structure called slot0.
Think of slot0() as a window into the pool's current state.
When you call:
pool.slot0();

The pool returns seven values, including:
sqrtPriceX96
tick

These two values help you understand the pool's current price.

- `sqrtPriceX96` → stores the current price in square-root format.
- `tick` → stores the current tick number.

Because `slot0` is publicly readable, anyone can check it using a block explorer like Etherscan or BaseScan.

| Value | Purpose |
|---|---|
| `sqrtPriceX96` | Represents the current price with high precision. |
| `tick` | Identifies the discrete price interval containing the current price. |

## 2. How Does the Current Tick Relate to Price?

Each tick represents a specific price level.

The formula is:

`Price at tick i = 1.0001^i`

The current price can be between two tick price levels, so the current tick generally tells us the lower tick boundary around the current price.

### Example: ETH/DAI Pool

Suppose the current tick is `81143`.

The price calculated from this tick is approximately:

`1 ETH ≈ 3340 DAI`

This means **1 ETH is worth around 3,340 DAI**.

Remember: the tick gives us an approximate price. To get the more precise current price, use `sqrtPriceX96`.

## 3. Why Do Token Decimals Matter?

Different tokens can use different numbers of decimal places.

For example:
- ETH uses 18 decimals.
- DAI uses 18 decimals.
- USDC uses 6 decimals.

This means their smallest units have different sizes.

For example:
- 1 ETH = `10^18` smallest units.
- 1 USDC = `10^6` smallest units.

**Important:** Uniswap V3's tick price is based on the ratio of the smallest units of `token1` to `token0`. It does not automatically show the human-readable token price.

## 4. Example: ETH/USDC Pool

Suppose the ETH/USDC pool has a current tick of `-195186`.

Using the tick formula:

`Price = 1.0001^(-195186)`

The result is approximately:

`0.0000000033389`

This number looks extremely small. Does it mean ETH is worth less than one USDC?

**No!** We must account for the difference in token decimals.

ETH has 18 decimals, while USDC has 6.

So we multiply the raw price by:

`10^(18 - 6) = 10^12`

Therefore:

`0.0000000033389 × 10^12 ≈ 3338.9`

The human-readable price is approximately:

`1 ETH ≈ 3338.9 USDC`

So ETH can be worth thousands of USDC even though the raw tick price is a very small number.

## 5. Quick Revision

- `slot0` stores the pool's current square-root price and current tick.
- A tick represents a specific price level.
- The current tick gives a useful estimate of the current price.
- `sqrtPriceX96` provides the more precise current price information.
- Token decimals affect how the raw price is converted into a human-readable price.
- ETH has 18 decimals; USDC has 6 decimals.
- Always account for token decimals when converting the raw pool price into a human-readable price.
