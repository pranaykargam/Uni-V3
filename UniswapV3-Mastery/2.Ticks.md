# Uniswap V3 — Ticks  ⭐️

## 1. What Are Ticks?

**Ticks are predefined price boundaries where liquidity can begin or end.**

They let Uniswap V3 track where active liquidity changes during a swap.

- **Tick spacing** depends on the pool's fee tier.
- Smaller spacing → more precise price boundaries.
- Larger spacing → fewer available boundaries.

## 2. Price vs. Ticks

Price moves continuously between ticks; it does not need to stop at each tick.

## 3. What Happens When a Tick Is Crossed?

Suppose three LPs have these ranges:

| LP | Price Range |
|---|---|
| Alice | $1,800–$2,200 |
| Bob | $1,900–$2,100 |
| Charlie | $2,000–$2,400 |

At $2,050, all three positions are active.

When the price crosses above $2,100, Bob's position becomes inactive, decreasing active liquidity.

**During a tick crossing, the pool updates active liquidity and continues the swap using the updated amount.** A tick crossing itself does not deposit or withdraw tokens.

### Key Takeaway

**Ticks define price boundaries. When a swap crosses an initialized tick, Uniswap V3 updates active liquidity to reflect the positions that are active at the new price.**

# Uniswap V3 — Tick-to-Price Formula ⭐️

### 1. Tick-to-Price Formula

**Formula:**

**p(i) = 1.0001ⁱ**

- **i** = tick index (integer).
- **p(i)** = price represented by that tick.
- Each increase of one tick multiplies the price by **1.0001**, approximately a **0.01% increase**.

Each tick index corresponds to a predefined price.

### 2. Tick Price Examples

| Tick Index (i) | Formula | Tick Price p(i) |
|---:|---|---:|
| -1 | 1.0001⁻¹ = 1 / 1.0001 | ≈ 0.99990001 |
| 0 | 1.0001⁰ | 1 |
| 1 | 1.0001¹ | 1.0001 |
| 2 | 1.0001² | 1.00020001 |

### 3. Positive vs. Negative Ticks

- **Negative tick (i < 0):** Price is below 1.
- **Zero tick (i = 0):** Price equals 1.
- **Positive tick (i > 0):** Price is above 1.

### 4. Current Price vs. Tick Price

The current price can move continuously between tick prices. It does not have to match a tick price exactly.

**Example:**

**1 < 1.00005 < 1.0001**

This means the current price of **1.00005** lies between tick 0 and tick 1.

- Price = **1.0000** → exactly tick 0.
- Price = **1.00005** → between ticks 0 and 1.
- Price = **1.0001** → exactly tick 1.

### 5. Tick Range

The permitted tick index range is:

**−887272 ≤ i ≤ 887272**

### Key Takeaway

Ticks are integer indices that represent predefined prices using the formula **p(i) = 1.0001ⁱ**. The current price can lie between tick prices. When the price crosses an initialized tick with a recorded liquidity change, the pool updates its active liquidity.


# Uniswap V3 — Token Price vs. Ticks ⭐️

### 1. Tick Price vs. Current Price

**Tick-to-price formula:**

`p(i) = 1.0001^i`

- **Tick:** An integer index representing a predefined price.
- **Tick price:** The price associated with a specific tick.
- **Current price:** The pool's actual price, which changes during swaps.

The current price can lie **between two tick prices**; it does not have to match a tick exactly.

**Analogy:** Ticks are like mileage markers on a road. A car moves continuously between them.

### 2. Why Is the Multiplier 1.0001?

`1.0001 = 1 + 0.0001`

`0.0001 = 0.01% = 1 basis point (bps)`

Each increase of one tick multiplies the previous tick price by **1.0001**, producing a **0.01% relative price increase**.

### 3. Relative vs. Absolute Price Change

- **Absolute change:** The numerical difference between two prices.
- **Relative change:** The difference compared with the starting price.

**Formula for neighboring ticks:**

`p(i + 1) = p(i) * 1.0001`

`Relative change = 0.01%`

The absolute difference varies with the starting price, but the relative difference remains approximately 0.01%.

### 4. Examples

| Tick transition | Starting price (approx.) | Absolute price change (approx.) | Relative change |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 10 → 11 | 1.00100045 | 0.00010010 | 0.01% |
| 100000 → 100001 | 22015.45605 | 2.20154561 | 0.01% |

**Key observation:** Higher prices have larger absolute differences between neighboring ticks, even though the percentage change remains the same.

### 5. Why Does This Matter?

Uniswap uses a proportional price step rather than a fixed absolute price difference. This allows neighboring ticks to represent consistent relative price changes across different price levels.

Ticks also mark where liquidity can be adjusted. **Crossing a tick changes active liquidity only when that initialized tick has a recorded liquidity change.**

### Quick Revision

- **Tick price:** `p(i) = 1.0001^i`
- **One tick upward:** Multiply price by `1.0001`.
- **Relative increase:** Approximately `0.01% = 1 bps`.
- **Absolute increase:** Depends on the starting price.
- **Current price:** Can lie between tick prices.
- **Liquidity adjustment:** Occurs at relevant initialized tick boundaries.
